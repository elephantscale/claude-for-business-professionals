# Claude for Business Professionals
*Data, reporting, and presentations — done in an afternoon, with Claude.*

© Elephant Scale

## Course Description

Most professionals have tried an AI chat once or twice and walked away thinking it's a
faster way to write email. It is much more than that — but only if someone shows you how to
point it at your **actual work**: the spreadsheet you dread, the report due Friday, the deck
you'll build the night before.

This is a hands-on, **code-free** course that does exactly that. In four short sessions,
participants take a real business dataset from raw numbers to a clear written report to a
draft presentation — entirely inside Claude, entirely in plain English. They leave able to
do it again on Monday, on their own material, safely.

The course is built around **analysis, reporting, and presentations** — the work these
people already do — not AI theory. Every session ends with hands-on practice on realistic
business material, and the four labs **chain**: the dataset you analyze in Session 2 becomes
the report in Session 3 and the deck in Session 4.

## Why This Course Matters

Professionals don't fail with AI because the tool is weak. They fail because:

- they never learned the difference between a lazy prompt and a working one
- they paste a number into a slide without checking whether the AI actually counted it
- they don't know what company data is safe to put in, so they either overshare or avoid it
- they treat it as a novelty instead of a repeatable part of their workflow

Each of those is a **skill**, not a talent — and each is trainable in an hour.

## Audience

- Non-technical professionals and managers — finance, operations, sales, HR, marketing, PMO
- Anyone who works with spreadsheets, writes reports, or builds presentations
- People who have a Claude account (or are about to) and want to actually use it

## Skill Level

Beginner. No coding, no math, no prior AI experience. If you can use a browser and a
spreadsheet, you're ready.

## Duration and Format

- **Four ~60-minute sessions**, hands-on throughout — delivered on consecutive days or as
  one half-day. Same content, same labs either way.
- Instructor-led, online. Up to 15 participants.
- Roughly half of every session is the participant working in Claude on business material.

## What Attendees Will Be Able to Do

By the end, each participant can independently use Claude to:

- Ask questions of their own spreadsheets and data, and get answers they can trust
- Turn raw numbers into a clear written report or executive summary
- Draft a presentation — structure, slide content, and speaker notes
- Write effective prompts and **reuse** them, so the gains outlast the class
- Do all of it **safely** — knowing what company data is and isn't OK to share

## Tooling — please read before delivery

This course runs on **Claude.ai**, not the API. The whole course is **Projects**, **file
upload**, **Artifacts**, and the **Analysis tool** (which runs real code on your spreadsheet
so the numbers are actually calculated, not guessed) — and those are Claude.ai features. An
API key and the developer Console give you none of them and are the wrong surface for this
audience.

**Recommended: Claude Team ("Claude for Work").** It gives every participant Projects, keeps
company data out of model training, and lets an admin provision seats centrally — the right
answer for a corporate room.

- **Individual Claude Pro** works identically for the labs if Team isn't ready in time.
- **Free** is too limited (Project and usage caps) — not recommended for the labs.
- **API keys / Console Workbench — not recommended.** No Projects, no Artifacts, no Analysis
  tool. If keys are truly the only option, tell the instructor before delivery; the labs
  would need to be redesigned and would lose their best parts.

See `labs/SETUP.md` for the exact setup and the data-safety rules.

# Session 1 — Getting Productive with Claude (≈60 min)
*Lab 1 — Your First Working Prompts*

- What Claude is, what it's genuinely good at, and where it needs a human check
- The anatomy of a working prompt: **context · task · format · example**
- Projects: a workspace that remembers your context, files, and instructions
- Uploading your material — documents, spreadsheets, notes
- Responsible use: what business data is safe to share, and what to keep out
- **Lab 1:** set up a Project, upload the course dataset, and turn a lazy prompt into a
  working one — on real business text

> The habit this session installs: *never send the first prompt you think of.* Add the
> context and the format, and the output changes completely — for free.

# Session 2 — Data Analysis in Plain English (≈60 min)
*Lab 2 — Analyze a Business Dataset End to End*

- Uploading a spreadsheet and simply **asking questions of it**
- Summaries, trends, comparisons, and outliers — no formulas required
- Cleaning and reshaping messy data by describing what you want
- Charts and tables — and the discipline of **sanity-checking Claude's numbers**
- The Analysis tool: when Claude *calculates* vs. when it *estimates*, and why it matters
- **Lab 2:** take the Kwanza Sugar Imports data from raw file to three findings you
  would put in front of your boss — including the one number that's wrong

> The habit this session installs: *the numbers feel like data — check the two that matter
> before they reach a slide.*

# Session 3 — From Analysis to a Clear Report (≈60 min)
*Lab 3 — Produce a Polished Report*

- Turning findings into an executive summary or a full written report
- Controlling **tone and length**; tailoring the same content to different audiences
- Editing and tightening a draft *with* Claude instead of starting over
- Reusable report templates and prompt "recipes" for consistency
- **Lab 3:** produce a one-page executive summary and a longer report from your Session 2
  findings — then retarget it for a different reader in one prompt

> The habit this session installs: *the words can be Claude's; the claims have to be yours.*

# Session 4 — Building a Presentation, and Making It Stick (≈60 min)
*Lab 4 — Draft a Short Deck*

- From report to slide **outline**: structure, flow, and the key message per slide
- Drafting slide content and **speaker notes** that sound like you
- Fast revisions — reordering, shortening, changing the angle
- A clean workflow **into PowerPoint / Google Slides** (and what Artifacts can and can't do)
- Wrap-up: building your **personal prompt library**, good habits, and where to go next
- **Lab 4:** draft a short deck from your Session 3 report, then start the prompt library you
  walk out with

> The habit this session installs: *a prompt you save is a task you never start from scratch
> again.*

# Labs

The four labs are the spine of the course. Every participant works on the **same shared
dataset** — Kwanza Sugar Imports, a fictional cane-sugar importer and distributor in Angola —
so the analysis, report, and deck build on each other and nobody gets stuck sourcing material.

| # | Lab | Session | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Your First Working Prompts | S1 | A Project set up + a rewritten "working" prompt |
| 2 | Analyze a Business Dataset | S2 | Three defensible findings (one error caught) |
| 3 | Produce a Polished Report | S3 | A one-page summary + a retargeted version |
| 4 | Draft a Short Deck | S4 | A slide outline with speaker notes + a prompt library |

All materials are in `course-materials/`. Fictional throughout — nothing to clear with
anyone's legal team.

# Tooling Summary

- **Claude.ai**, in a browser — no installation, no code.
- **Claude Team ("Claude for Work")** recommended; **Pro** is a fine individual fallback.
- The material is written around Claude's real features — **Projects, file upload, Artifacts,
  the Analysis tool** — because that is what participants will use at their desks next week.

# Positioning

This is not an "intro to AI" talk. It is a **working session**: four hours that turn Claude
from a thing people have heard about into a tool they use on their own numbers, their own
reports, and their own decks — safely, and repeatably.

> You arrive with a spreadsheet you'd rather not open. You leave with the analysis, the
> report, and the deck — and the prompts to do it again.
