# From Analysis to a Clear Report

Session 3

Elephant Scale

## The Job This Session

You have three findings. Now make writing that a busy executive will actually read — and can
act on.

The good news: the hard part (the analysis) is done. This session is about **shaping** it —
for a reader, at a length, in a voice.

Notes:

Reassure the room: they already did the thinking in Session 2. Writing is now a
transformation task, which is exactly what Claude is best at.

---

## Structure Before Prose

Don't ask for "a report." Ask for a **structure**:

> A one-sentence headline · "What's working" / "What needs attention" · one recommendation
> with its risk · under 300 words.

Structure first means you can fix the shape before you polish the words.

Notes:

The parallel to Session 2's "orient before you ask" and Session 4's "outline before slides."
Same discipline three times: get the skeleton right before the surface.

---

## Tone, Length, Audience — All Just Specified

The same facts become three different documents depending on the reader:

* **The CFO** — numbers, risk, the decision. Terse.
* **The port managers** — what it means for *them*, not a scorecard.
* **The all-hands** — plain, reassuring, no jargon.

You don't rewrite. You say who it's for, and re-run.

Notes:

This is the highest-leverage idea in the session. Demo the retarget live if you can — one
sentence of instruction, a completely different document. The lab has them do it.

---

## Edit With It — Don't Start Over

Never regenerate a whole draft to fix one line. **Steer** it:

* *"Tighten this section to three sentences, more direct, less hedging."*
* *"Rewrite the recommendation as an action with an owner and a deadline."*
* *"Cut every sentence a skeptic could call vague."*

Each instruction changes one thing. That's editing, not gambling.

Notes:

Common bad habit: people re-run the whole prompt hoping for a better draft, losing the good
parts. Show the surgical edit — it's faster and keeps what worked.

---

## The Honesty Pass

The pass most people skip and shouldn't:

> *"Flag every sentence stating something not directly supported by the data — any number,
> claim, or cause that was inferred rather than found."*

Then **you** decide which inferences to keep. That decision is why you can defend the report.

Notes:

This is Session 3's version of Session 2's sanity-check. "The words can be Claude's; the
claims have to be yours." Protect this in the lab even under time pressure — it's the
professional core.

---

## Templates and Recipes — So It Sticks

Turn a prompt that worked into a reusable one:

> *"Turn my executive-summary prompt into a template with [BRACKETS] for the audience, length,
> and subject — something I can paste and fill in next time."*

Save it. Next month's report is now a five-minute job.

Notes:

Previews Session 4's prompt library. The theme of the whole course: capture what worked so
the gains outlast the class.
