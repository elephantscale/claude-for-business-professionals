## The Last Mile

Report done. Now the deck — the thing you'll actually stand up and present.

Same move as before: **structure first, polish last.** Get the story and the flow right, then
worry about slides.

Notes:

By now the "structure first" pattern is familiar — orient, then structure, then surface. Name

---

## Report to Slide Outline

Don't ask for "slides." Ask for an **outline**:

> One slide per key message · 5–6 slides · each with a **takeaway title** (not a topic) and
> 3 tight bullets · start with the headline, end with the ask.

A "topic" title is *"East Region."* A **takeaway** title is *"East needs a decision now."*

Notes:

The takeaway-vs-topic distinction is the whole slide-writing lesson. A deck of takeaway titles
tells the story even if you only read the titles. Demo the difference.

---

## Speaker Notes That Sound Like You

The slide is the visual; the notes are what you **say**:

> *"For each slide, ~2 sentences of speaker notes — what I'd say out loud, conversational,
> first person. Add the 'so what', don't just read the bullets."*

If it sounds like a press release, tell it: *"less corporate, like I'm talking to colleagues."*

Notes:

Voice is the one thing only they can judge. Encourage reading a note aloud — the ear catches
what the eye misses. This is where "sounds like you" is won or lost.

---

## Fast Revisions — The Real Workflow

Decks change at the last minute. That's a feature here, not a problem:

* *"Cut to 4 slides, merge the two region slides."*
* *"Reframe the East slide from a problem into a decision."*
* *"Stronger close — one clear ask, not a summary."*

Draft fast, reshape on demand. **The speed is the point.**

Notes:

Contrast with the old way — an hour of dragging text boxes. The value proposition is
iteration speed: try three versions in the time one used to take.

---

## Into PowerPoint / Google Slides

Claude drafts the **content**; you move it into your tool:

* Ask for it **as paste-ready text** — title, bullets, then a "Notes:" line per slide
* **PowerPoint Outline View** turns a pasted outline into slides
* **Google Slides** — paste titles/bullets into the outline, notes into speaker notes

Notes:

Keep this practical and concrete — this is the step people get stuck on. The paste-ready-text
approach is the most reliable across tools. Walk it once on screen.

---

## The Honest Limit

Claude builds the **words, the structure, the notes** — 90% of the deck, in minutes.

It does **not** build your company's branded, pixel-perfect template. You do the last 10% —
theme, logo, polish — in your slide tool.

> That trade — minutes of content for minutes of polish — is almost always worth it.

Notes:

Head off the "but it's not real slides" disappointment before it lands. Framed as a trade, it
reads as the win it is. Manage the expectation and the room stays happy.

## Making It Stick — Your Prompt Library

The real takeaway isn't a document. It's the **prompts** that made them:

* Analyze a spreadsheet · Findings → report · Retarget for a new reader · Report → deck
* Ask Claude: *"pick the 5 prompts that did the most work and write each as a reusable
  template with [BRACKETS]."*
* Save them. That's next month's work, already half-done.

Notes:

This is the whole point of the course, restated. Hand out / point to
`course-materials/prompt-library-template.md`. Protect time for it in the lab.

## Good Habits to Leave With

* **Verify** — check the numbers that matter; run the honesty pass
* **Protect data** — the screenshot test, every time
* **Iterate** — never accept the first draft; steer it
* **Structure first** — orient, outline, then polish
* **Save what works** — a prompt reused is a task never restarted

Notes:

The five habits are the course in one slide. If someone remembers only this slide, they got
their money's worth.
