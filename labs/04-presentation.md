# Lab 4 — Draft a Short Deck

**Session 4** · Individual, in Claude · ~30 minutes

---

## Purpose

Turn your Session 3 report into a **draft presentation** — a slide outline with one clear
message per slide and speaker notes that sound like you — then get it into PowerPoint or
Google Slides. Finish by starting the **prompt library** you walk out with, so today's gains
survive contact with Monday.

The discipline being trained: **a prompt you save is a task you never start from scratch
again.**

## Before you start

- Same Project. Claude has your report from Lab 3.
- Decide your target: **PowerPoint** or **Google Slides** (the export step differs slightly).

## Step 1 — Report to slide outline (7 min)

Don't ask for "slides" — ask for **structure** first:

> ```
> Turn my executive summary into a slide outline for a 5-minute update to leadership.
> One slide per key message, 5–6 slides max. For each slide give:
> - a short, specific title (the takeaway, not a topic)
> - 3 bullet points, tight
> Start with the headline finding; end with the recommendation and its risk.
> ```

Check the **flow**: does slide 1 land the most important thing, and does it build to the ask?
If not, reorder before you add any detail — cheap now, expensive later.

## Step 2 — Speaker notes that sound like you (6 min)

The slides are the visual; the notes are what you say:

> ```
> For each slide, write speaker notes — what I'd actually say out loud, about two
> sentences per slide, conversational, first person. Don't just read the bullets;
> add the "so what" for each. Match a plain, direct speaking style.
> ```

Read one aloud. If it sounds like a press release, tell Claude: *"less corporate, more like
I'm talking to colleagues"* — and re-run. You're tuning voice, which only you can judge.

## Step 3 — Fast revisions (5 min)

Presentations get changed at the last minute. Practice it:

> - *"The leadership team is short on time — cut this to 4 slides and merge the two
>   port slides."*
> - *"Reframe the Namibe slide from a problem into a decision we need them to make."*
> - *"Give me a stronger closing slide — one clear ask, not a summary."*

This is the real workflow: draft fast, then reshape on demand. The speed is the point.

## Step 4 — Get it into PowerPoint / Google Slides (5 min)

Claude drafts the content; you move it into your slide tool. The clean paths:

> - **Fastest, reliable:** ask — *"Format the whole deck as text I can paste into slides:
>   each slide as a title line, then bullets, then a 'Notes:' line."* Then paste slide by
>   slide. Titles and bullets land where they should.
> - **PowerPoint outline import:** ask for the outline in a form you can paste into
>   PowerPoint's **Outline View**, which creates the slides for you.
> - **Google Slides:** paste each slide's title and bullets into the outline/speaker-notes;
>   or paste into a Google Doc first and use it as your working script.

> **Honest limit:** Claude builds *content*, not your company's branded, pixel-perfect
> template. It gets you 90% there — the words, the structure, the notes — in minutes. You do
> the last 10% (theme, logo, polish) in your slide tool. That trade is almost always worth it.

## Step 5 — Build your prompt library (5 min)

The thing that makes today stick. Open `course-materials/prompt-library-template.md` (or a
notes doc) and capture the **3–5 prompts you actually used** this week that you'll reuse:

> Ask Claude to help: *"Look back over our whole conversation across these sessions. Pick the
> 5 prompts that did the most work — analysis, the report, the retarget, the deck — and write
> each as a clean reusable template with [BRACKETS] for what changes."*

Paste those into your library. That's your takeaway: not notes about Claude, but **working
tools** you'll use next week.

## Deliverable

- A 4–6 slide outline with speaker notes, in your slide tool.
- A personal prompt library with at least three reusable prompts.

## Facilitator watch-fors

- People who ask for "slides" and get a wall of text. Redirect to **structure first** (Step
  1) — titles as takeaways, then flow, then detail.
- Frustration that it's "not real slides." Reset the expectation (Step 4's honest limit):
  content in minutes, polish in your tool. That's the win, not a loss.
- The prompt library gets rushed at the end. Protect five minutes for it — it's the entire
  reason the class outlasts the day.

## If you finish early

Ask Claude to draft the **email that sends the deck** — subject line, two-sentence body, and
a one-line note on what you want feedback on. The full chain, desk-ready: data → report →
deck → the message that ships it.
